William Stewart Halsted

The father of modern surgery

William Stewart Halsted was born in New York City in 1852. He studied medicine at Columbia's College of Physicians and Surgeons and trained in Europe with some of the great surgeons of his time. Back in New York, in 1884, he experimented with cocaine as a local anesthetic. In 1889 he became the first chief of surgery at Johns Hopkins Hospital, where he changed surgery forever.

His legacy

  • Local anesthesia by nerve block (dentistry & general surgery).
  • He introduced rubber gloves in the operating room.
  • He advanced gallbladder surgery, hernia repair, the radical mastectomy, vascular surgery and thyroid surgery.
  • He created the surgical residency, a training system still used today.
  • Among his trainees: Harvey Cushing (neurosurgery), Hugh Young (urology), William Baer (orthopedic surgery), Samuel Crowe (otolaryngology) and Mont Reid (vascular surgery).

His last days

In 1922, Halsted was operated on for gallstones at his own hospital, Johns Hopkins, by surgeons he had trained. He died a few weeks later, on September 7, 1922, of complications after the operation.

Conclusion

This presentation is not meant to exalt the benefits or the harms of drugs. Instead, it is meant to celebrate human greatness. Halsted kept his struggle hidden for most of his life, while he continued to transform surgery. Even from the depths of the abyss, a person can keep moving forward.
